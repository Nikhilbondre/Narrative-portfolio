DON'T GO BACK

About the story

You're Neel, an office worker waiting outside your building in the rain. Your umbrella is upstairs on the fourth floor. Your phone buzzes with a message from your own number telling you not to go back. A brown dog appears exactly when the message predicts, and a figure wearing your orange jacket waves from the fourth-floor window.

The interactive story has two main routes: obey the warning and take Bus 612, or go upstairs to discover who is wearing your jacket. Your first reply to the message is remembered and changes a line later in the story. Going upstairs reveals another position in the loop: one Neel becomes the person sending the warning to the next.

Design intent

  • Remembered choice: the first answer comes back in dialogue on the bus route.
  • Two perspectives: one route lets Neel leave; the other places him at the window sending the warning.
  • Repetition as evidence: the forty-one conversations make the loop legible through a repeated detail rather than a long explanation.
  • An emotional reveal: the message shifts from an order into a desperate request from someone trying to keep the next Neel from becoming him.
The warning isn't only about where to go. It's about who you become when you go back.
