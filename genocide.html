PROJECT 01 / GAME NARRATIVE + WORLDBUILDING

GENOCIDE

A dark fantasy action-RPG about a monk's pursuit of demons — and the violence that gradually reshapes the person doing the hunting.

REVENGE · CONSEQUENCE · THE CYCLE OF VIOLENCE

Project overview

GENOCIDE follows a monk who sets out to eliminate demons after an attack destroys his family and monastery. He was raised to believe demons are lost souls who should be guided back to heaven. After witnessing the massacre, that belief fractures, and his journey pushes him toward revenge while steadily eroding his will to live.

The concept is planned as a bounded, chapter-based journey, inspired by the connected level design and demanding boss encounters of Soulslike games: 13 chapters and 15 boss encounters, with the fifteenth boss serving as the final encounter. The world uses interconnected, bounded areas rather than a single seamless open-world map. The setting draws on feudal-Japan-inspired fantasy with planned visual and cultural influences from Nepal, Tibet, and several South and East Asian regions. This is an independent concept/design project; descriptions below distinguish proposed systems from implemented gameplay.

Opening cinematic — faith breaks

The opening uses contrast instead of a lore dump. It begins on a black screen as the protagonist recalls what people believe about God and the teaching he grew up with: demons are lost souls, and all they need is a path to heaven.

01 / The massacre

Small demons overrun a settlement and attack people trying to escape. The sequence is staged to show the horror and brutality of the attack.

02 / The wounded monk

A severely wounded female monk continues fighting until she collapses and her sword strikes the ground.

03 / A sanctuary destroyed

The camera travels through the ruined monastery and passes dead monks before resting on blood-stained god statues.

04 / The lesson

A childhood memory shows an old monk teaching: “Do not kill anything that has been created by nature. If it is meant to die, nature itself will take its life.” When a student asks about demons, the monk replies: “Nature will look after them too.”

05 / Awakening

The mother's desperate scream — “PLEASE! KILL THEM!” — cuts into the present. The protagonist wakes among the ruins, remembers the massacre, and thinks: “I suppose the elders were wrong.” Player control begins.

Featured boss — Genjo, “The Butcher”

Genjo is a large, intimidating fighter who loves flowers. His daughter was killed by people who held a grudge against him. He knows she is dead, but he cannot emotionally accept the loss and continues to speak as if she is sleeping beneath a tree in the Valley of Flowers. The encounter contrasts his brutal combat style with the imagined peaceful moment he is trying to protect.

Encounter overview

  • Memory: Genjo and his daughter play in a flower field. She rests with her head on his lap; the same composition returns to reveal her motionless body and blood at her mouth.
  • Present: Genjo sits in the flowers beside his weapon and a lone tree. He notices the protagonist and says: “Hah... sorry. My daughter's sleeping under that tree. Keep your voice down.”
  • Combat: After asking the protagonist to leave, Genjo attacks with a sudden dash and a heavy strike. His size makes the speed unexpected, but the movement is intended to provide a fair visual cue.
  • Dialogue progression: “Please, leave. I don't want my daughter to wake up.” As his patience breaks: “You should have left when I told you to.” At low health: “No! Not in front of my daughter!”
  • Player choice: A respectful death ends quietly with “Finally, I can join her... Thank you.” A brutal execution uses a violent finish and does not include that final thank-you; exact staging remains under development.
  • Optional discovery: Investigating the tree reveals the daughter's remains, a flower, a note explaining that she was killed because of a grudge against her father, and a small loot reward.
“Killing is the easy part... living with the weight of killing someone is not easy.”

Environmental storytelling — the shrines

Three shrine engravings show the balance of power changing across time: first humans defeat monsters; then monsters begin to take over; finally monsters are shown winning. The images offer lore and potential hints about bosses without a character explaining their meaning out loud. Smaller visual clues can also reward attention by pointing toward a hidden cave with basic loot.

The companion — a silent shapeshifter

The player eventually gains a nonverbal slime companion. It uses spatial magic to relocate the protagonist up to five times per battle and stores up to 36 items, like a portable chest. It cannot speak; it communicates by drawing on the ground with a small wooden branch, which it retrieves from its spatial storage. It needs water to survive. Its role is not direct combat, but utility, personality, and an emotional bond built through behaviour rather than dialogue.

Narrative design pillars

  • Bosses as character stories: encounter behaviour and combat dialogue express inner conflict.
  • Choices with emotional framing: the Genjo ending changes how the player treats a defeated enemy.
  • Story through spaces: shrines, trees, corpses, and visual traces carry information.
  • Mechanics reinforcing theme: the proposed concept links survival pressure, combat aftermath, boss adaptation, and the protagonist's changing identity to the narrative's cycle of violence.

Project status: original concept and narrative design in progress. The project was presented at IGDC 2025 to AWS representatives and other investors. Proposed systems described here are concepts, not claims of completed implementation.
